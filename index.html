Full-scale design targets

01The gap

Vertical lift is cheap. Speed and range are not.

Multirotors and jetpacks lift off anywhere, but they cruise at 30 to 40 mph and run out of energy within minutes. Aircraft that fly fast and far need a runway, or cost and maintain like a helicopter. No low-cost system today takes off vertically, flies at 150 mph, carries a person’s weight and covers tens of miles.

02The wing

Lift off like a drone. Cross distance like an aircraft.

The Manta Wing stands on its tail to take off and land, then pitches over and flies on its wing. Hover only lasts long enough to get up and get down. In between, the wing carries the load, so the trip runs at aircraft speed on a fraction of the energy.

It is optionally manned. The same airframe flies itself, carries a load, or carries a person.

03Primary mission

Fly in empty. Fly out loaded.

The wing launches from base with no one on it and flies itself to a contested landing zone. A soldier straps in, or cargo is swapped, and it flies back out. Rapid insertion and rapid exfiltration, of a person or a load, over a round trip of 40 miles or more.

04Control

One wing, three levels of control.

The flight computer decides how much of the flying the person does. A soldier who has never flown can be carried out. A trained pilot can fly it by hand. The protections that matter most never switch off.
